# MITRE ATT&CK Technique T1498: Network Denial of Service

Adversaries may perform Network Denial of Service (DoS) attacks to degrade
or block the availability of targeted resources to users. Network DoS can
be performed by exhausting the network bandwidth services rely on, or by
depleting connection-handling resources on the target host through a high
volume of connection or packet requests.

## Sub-techniques
- T1498.001 Direct Network Flood: Sending high volumes of traffic (packets,
  connections, or requests) directly at a target to consume available
  bandwidth or connection capacity, e.g. SYN floods, UDP floods, ICMP floods.
- T1498.002 Reflection Amplification: Using intermediary servers to reflect
  and amplify traffic toward a victim, increasing volume beyond what the
  attacker's own bandwidth would allow.

## Common Indicators
- An abnormally high number of flow records or connection attempts from one
  source toward a single destination within a short time window.
- Many short-lived or incomplete connections (e.g. SYN packets with no
  completed handshake) arriving in rapid succession.
- A sharp, sustained increase in packet or connection volume compared to
  the destination's normal baseline traffic.

## Detection Guidance
Defenders can detect network DoS activity by monitoring for abnormal spikes
in flow/connection counts or packet volume toward a single destination
within short time windows, rather than relying solely on inspecting
individual packets or flows in isolation, since any single packet in a
flood may appear unremarkable on its own. Rate limiting, SYN cookies,
upstream traffic scrubbing, and capacity planning are common mitigations.

## Relevant Mitigations
- Rate limiting and connection throttling at the network edge.
- SYN cookies or similar techniques to reduce the cost of handling
  incomplete TCP handshakes.
- Upstream DDoS scrubbing services for volumetric attacks.
- Monitoring and alerting on abnormal connection/packet volume.
